# Week-7-AnalystLab-Africa-Assignment
Week 7 focused on testing, refinement and validation of my Data Analytics outputs developed during Week 6 assignment. The same HealthConnect appointment dataset was retained, and the existing Power BI dashboard was treated as the starting point.

Week 7 of the AnalystLab Africa Experience Lab was a continuation of my HealthConnect Clinic Experience Lab project.

Rather than creating a new dashboard, I focused on testing, validating, refining, and re-testing the analytical work developed during Week 6.

The main workflow for this week was:

Test → Identify Issues → Refine → Re-test → Validate → Document

---

What I Worked On

1. KPI Validation

I reviewed and tested the key KPIs used in my HealthConnect Power BI dashboard, including:

* Total Appointments
* Attendance Rate
* No-show Rate
* Cancellation Rate
* Total No-shows
* Reminder Coverage

The Week 6 baseline included:

| KPI                |  Value |
| ------------------ | -----: |
| Total Appointments |  5,000 |
| Attendance Rate    | 46.28% |
| No-show Rate       | 48.46% |
| Cancellation Rate  |  5.26% |
| Total No-shows     |  2,423 |
| Reminder Coverage  | 72.68% |

This helped me understand the importance of validating dashboard outputs rather than simply assuming that calculated values are correct.

---

2. Power BI Filter & Slicer Testing

I tested the existing dashboard filters and slicers to determine whether they were interacting correctly with the relevant visuals.

The testing included areas such as:

* Reminder Status
* Gender
* Age Group

I learned how to observe the effect of filters on KPI cards and analytical visuals and identify situations where a visual may not respond as expected.

---

3. Visual Interaction Testing

I tested important analytical visuals under different filter selections.

This helped me understand that a dashboard needs to be tested not only for appearance but also for functionality and interaction.

I reviewed whether:

* Charts responded to filters.
* KPI values updated appropriately.
* Analytical relationships remained interpretable.
* Visuals continued to communicate the intended message.

---

4. Analytical Finding Validation

I revisited important findings from Week 6 involving:

* Booking lead time
* Previous no-show history
* Reminder status
* Distance to clinic
* Waiting time
* Combined risk segments

The purpose was to determine whether the findings remained supported when examined through different dashboard segments and filters.

---

5. Power BI Dashboard Refinement

After testing the dashboard, I reviewed areas where the presentation could be improved.

The refinement process focused on areas such as:

* Making visual titles clearer.
* Improving KPI labels.
* Reviewing visual spacing and layout.
* Checking whether filters interacted with the correct visuals.
* Removing and adjusting unclear dashboard elements where necessary.

The goal was not to rebuild the Week 6 dashboard, but to improve the existing analytical solution based on testing.

---

6. Re testing After Refinement

One of the most important lessons from Week 7 was that making a change is not the end of the process.

After refinement, I re-tested important:

* KPIs
* Filters
* Visual interactions
* Analytical outputs

This reinforced the importance of the cycle:

Test → Finding → Action → Re test → Validated Improvement

Biggest Takeaway

My biggest takeaway from this week is that:

A dashboard is not finished when it looks good. It is finished when its calculations, interactions, findings, and interpretation have been tested and validated toward thinking more like an analyst responsible for the quality and reliability of the information being communicated.
